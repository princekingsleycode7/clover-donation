Field Dispatch · Northern Rift Valley

The Turkana Solar Borehole & Clean Water Initiative

In Turkana, water is not merely a utility—it is the difference between children staying in primary school and walking through scorching desert heat.

Severe cyclical drought across northern Turkana County forces over 14,000 pastoralist families and primary school children to walk up to 18 kilometers daily in search of contaminated riverbed water. Our initiative constructs deep solar-powered aquifer boreholes, hygienic water kiosks, and drip-irrigated community seed gardens that provide continuous, safe water for generations.
Unlike fragile diesel generator pumps that fail when fuel runs dry, modern high-efficiency solar submersible systems operate reliably under the intense equatorial sun with near-zero operating costs. Each borehole taps pristine deep aquifers protected from surface contaminants, eliminating cholera and bacterial infections.
Students at Kalokol Primary School with clean drinking water. School attendance increased by 38% after borehole installation.

Why Solar Aquifer Pumps?

  • ✓ No Ongoing Fuel Burden: Free, abundant solar power means the well pumps continuously throughout the hottest daylight hours.
  • ✓ Deep Aquifer Protection: Drilling to 160–200 meters isolates the supply from seasonal surface droughts and agricultural runoff.
  • ✓ Community Ownership: Trained village water custodians collect nominal micro-fees for livestock to maintain a permanent maintenance fund.
